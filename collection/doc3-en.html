SCGA and SCRA

Two quantitative futures programs built around a permanent equity core: sources of return, mechanics, risk management, and access structure — including the establishment of dedicated investment vehicles

1.Purpose and audience

This document presents the two programs of Core One Asset Management — SCGA (S&P 500 Core GTVP Allocation) and SCRA (S&P 500 Core Relative Allocation) — for evaluation by qualified and professional investors. Both are quantitative programs executed by algorithms: investment decisions are generated by rules formalized in advance, developed entirely in-house on the basis of academic finance and empirical market research; signal evaluation is daily, execution is systematic — with no manager discretion over the signals — and a no-trade discipline means the portfolio is only traded when the change indicated by the model is material, containing turnover and costs.

One prior delimitation: this document is informational in nature and does not constitute a public offering, a solicitation to buy, or investment advice; the definitive terms of any investment will be established exclusively in the corresponding contractual documentation.

2.Philosophy: manage the risk, don't replace the index

Traditional active management is organized around one objective: to beat a benchmark by abandoning it — through security selection, arbitrage, alternative assets, or the usual hedge-fund strategies. The long-run evidence on that approach is adverse and consistent: most such programs underperform the index they attempt to beat, because the set of active managers constitutes the market itself and, after costs, necessarily falls behind. The problem is not a lack of talent, but the framing: the attempt is made to win on the terrain — predicting the direction of prices — where the edge is scarcest and least persistent.

These programs invert the framing. Rather than forgoing the equity premium in order to try to beat it from outside, they retain exposure to the benchmark as a permanent position and apply quantitative capability to the variable that is genuinely manageable systematically: volatility and risk. The primary source of return remains the structural equity premium — the most reliable over the long run; the added value comes not from guessing what will rise, but from sizing exposure to the market regime, so that realized risk stays within a controlled band and compounding is less penalized by drawdowns. It is, in essence, the logic of a hedge fund applied not to replacing the index, but to owning it more efficiently.

3.Access structure and vehicle

Current status: separately managed accounts. Both programs are offered through separately managed accounts (SMA) at Interactive Brokers, with a suggested minimum of approximately US$500,000 per program — the amount above which positions can be expressed in whole contracts with acceptable granularity and execution and financing costs remain proportionate. Capacity is large: the markets traded are among the most liquid listed instruments in the world, so the strategies can scale materially before market impact becomes a consideration.

Objective: the dedicated investment vehicle. The SMA structure is appropriate for the current phase — maximum transparency and verifiability, account by account. Efficient implementation at scale requires, however, the establishment of dedicated collective investment vehicles, designed jurisdiction by jurisdiction: a vehicle restores part of the tax deferral on the futures activity — which in individual accounts is taxed annually in most European jurisdictions — professionalizes administration, custody, and audit, and mutualizes execution costs among participants. The establishment of these vehicles is the objective of the current phase, and the firm is open to conversations with early anchor allocators, proprietary trading groups, and joint-venture partners, in both managed accounts and fund structures.

Both programs launch calibrated to a target negative volatility of 10%–20% annualized and a maximum leverage of 300%. These two parameters are adjustable per investor: a mandate can be set to lower volatility and lower maximum leverage, a more defensive profile, or keep the launch values, according to its risk tolerance and horizon. The rest of the mechanics is common to both configurations.

4.SCGA — S&P 500 Core GTVP Allocation

Strategy. SCGA is a tax-aware, long-biased program that holds a permanent physical equity core — a UCITS ETF — and manages market exposure through a capital-efficient futures sleeve (in the literature, an overlay). The objective is to compound with the equity market while deferring the realization of capital gains on the core, adjusting risk through the futures sleeve rather than by trading the underlying. Net exposure is held within a controlled, configurable range around an equity baseline.

Source of return. Two, both structural. The first is the equity premium — the long-run compensation for owning productive corporate assets — which the physical core captures directly and permanently. The second is tax: every sale of an appreciated asset crystallizes a tax liability that permanently leaves the compounding base; by adjusting exposure with derivatives instead of selling the underlying, the deferred tax remains invested and compounding in the participant's favor. Holding the core indefinitely and shaping risk with the futures sleeve is, at once, an investment decision and an after-tax efficiency decision.

Universe and instruments. The physical core is a broad, low-cost UCITS ETF — for example, CSPX on the S&P 500, listed on the London Stock Exchange and denominated in dollars — chosen for its regulatory suitability, low ongoing cost, and appropriateness for an indefinite hold. Exposure is adjusted with the S&P 500 E-mini (ES) and Micro E-mini (MES) futures, and their sizing is calibrated continuously against the correlation levels between the future and the spot selected as the core, so that each adjustment faithfully reflects the portfolio's effective exposure. The primary formulation of the program is the one described here: an S&P 500 spot core and an ES/MES futures sleeve. Secondarily, the same objective can be implemented entirely with futures — always through an investment vehicle that permits it — and on a global index instead of the S&P 500; these are instrumentation variants that do not alter the thesis.

Mechanics. Three elements. Core discipline: the physical position is bought and held permanently; its share count can only grow. Futures sleeve: it sits on top of the fully-invested core and moves net exposure up or down around it — adding exposure in calm, favorable conditions and reducing it when conditions deteriorate, with a size bounded relative to total capital. Reinvestment: because futures settle in cash daily, realized sleeve gains accumulate as real cash; on a regular cadence, cash above a maintained buffer is directed to the purchase of additional core shares. Sleeve losses are carried against that buffer, without affecting the physical position.

Risk management. Risk is managed exclusively through the futures sleeve, which reduces net exposure in adverse conditions — reduces it, does not eliminate it: the program participates in bear markets. The cash buffer absorbs the sleeve's ordinary losses before any margin borrowing against the pledged core is required, and both net exposure and gross leverage are bounded.

5.SCRA — S&P 500 Core Relative Allocation

Strategy. SCRA is a long-biased futures program built around a permanent U.S. large-cap equity core, with dynamic tilts of a capped portion of the portfolio into a diversified set of liquid futures markets when they outperform the core on a risk-adjusted basis. The objective is to build an efficient product that, on a risk-adjusted basis, significantly outperforms equities — capturing their premium while quantitative management keeps realized risk within a controlled band through the cycle. The tactical sleeve rotates around the core, not in its place.

Source of return. Three, documented and structural. The first is the equity premium, which the program captures permanently by remaining net long equity at all times. The second is diversification across genuinely different premia: the duration of the rates curve, precious metals, and digital assets respond to the economic cycle differently from equities, and combining them under a common risk budget improves return per unit of risk. The third is the persistence of relative leadership: when a market outperforms the others in a sustained, risk-adjusted way, it tends to keep doing so for a time, because institutional capital reallocates slowly; tilting the portfolio toward that leadership adds return without abandoning the equity anchor.

Universe and instruments. Ten regulated, USD-denominated futures contracts, spanning four economic roles: equity indices (S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, Russell 2000), real assets (gold, silver), the U.S. rates curve (2-, 5-, and 10-year notes and the long bond), and digital assets (bitcoin). Each instrument represents a distinct macroeconomic exposure that belongs in a long-horizon allocation; short-term positioning vehicles and narrow sub-sector contracts are deliberately excluded. The S&P 500 is the permanent core and the benchmark against which every alternative's relative performance is judged.

Mechanics. Three elements. Signal: each alternative market is continuously assessed on its recent risk-adjusted performance relative to the core; markets that persistently outperform it earn allocation, and those that do not revert toward the core — the intent is to tilt toward durable leadership, not to predict direction. Sizing: positions are scaled by their recent downside volatility, so that each contributes a comparable amount of risk, and overall exposure is set by a dynamic-leverage mechanism that expands in calm, high-conviction conditions and contracts in stressed or weak-signal conditions. Cadence: signals are evaluated every trading day, and the portfolio is only traded when the implied change is material.

Risk management. Risk is managed at the position and portfolio level. Each position is sized to a comparable risk contribution through downside-volatility targeting, so that no single market can dominate the outcome. The equity core always holds the residual weight; gross leverage is bounded, and the tactical sleeve is capped so it cannot rotate away from the equity anchor.
